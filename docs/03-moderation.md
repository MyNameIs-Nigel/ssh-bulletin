# Bulletin moderation proposal

## Assessment of v1

- The SSH resolver authenticates direct players by key and accepts player fingerprints from configured Arcade proxy keys. An SSH username only suggests a display handle. SQLite already enforces case-insensitive handle uniqueness on registration and profile changes.
- The thread view uses `OP` for the opening post. There is no persistent owner, admin role, report queue, or action history.
- Posting runs through the board service and SQLite. The SSH layer limits sessions per key and requests per network address, while the Arcade lobby applies its own public-IP limit. Neither limits posts made from an open session. The Arcade v2 bridge forwards identity and terminal metadata, so the bulletin sees the lobby's address for every proxied player.

## Forum patterns and choices

- Discourse puts flags into a staff review queue, and Reddit gives moderators a queue with context and review actions. A Bulletin report records a reason and enters a private queue without changing visibility. A moderator can dismiss the reports or remove the post. Keeping the decision with a comment makes the outcome understandable to the next moderator. [Discourse moderation guide](https://meta.discourse.org/t/discourse-moderation-guide/63116) · [Reddit moderation queue](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/15484440494356-Moderation-Queue)
- Keep the original content for staff review and allow restoration. Removed posts are replaced with placeholders. Removing the opening post also masks the thread title and closes it to new replies until restored. Staff can review the current removed items and a paginated, append-only history.
- SSH public-key authentication proves possession of a private key. A handle can change, so store roles against the underlying key identity. The owner looks up a registered handle and enters the complete SHA-256 fingerprint supplied by that player before granting or revoking admin access. [RFC 4252, section 7](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc4252#section-7)
- OpenSSH uses per-source connection controls, and RFC 6269 describes the collateral effects of treating a shared IP as one user. Give directly connected players a shared concurrent-session cap per IPv4 address or IPv6 /64. For all ingress, apply posting quotas to each key and a shared quota to new accounts. The live lobby's existing per-IP limit still governs its public listener. A network can represent several machines, and a machine can change networks. [OpenSSH sshd_config](https://man.openbsd.org/sshd_config#PerSourceMaxStartups) · [RFC 6269](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc6269)

## Rollout and workflow

- Production startup requires `BULLETIN_OWNER_FINGERPRINT`. Bulletin reserves that key before accepting sessions, and an existing database's recorded owner must match it. Follow the [launch steps](01-arcade-onboarding.md#reserve-and-verify-the-super-admin) to verify the account. In development mode, leaving the setting empty lets the first authenticated terminal session claim `SU`. For a database upgraded from before roles, that fallback uses the earliest registered player.
- `SU` marks the super admin, `A` marks an admin, and `OP` still identifies the opening post in a thread. A staff member can display both flairs. The profile shows the player's own fingerprint.
- In a thread, select a post and press `!` to report it. In chat, select a message with `tab` and open its `o` menu. The `o` menu on another player's profile also has a Report action. Give a short reason. Players can submit up to five reports across these targets in ten minutes.
- Open **Moderation** with `m` or the jump menu. The **Queue**, **History**, and **Removed** tabs are available to admins and the owner. Enter opens details, including the reported chat message and up to two nearby messages on either side, or the profile's handle and bio at the time of the report. Use `d` to dismiss, `x` to remove a post or chat message, and `u` to restore a removed post. On profile reports, use `b` to clear the reported bio or `n` to require a different handle. Staff decisions require a reason and appear in History. The owner can use `a` and `v` in the **Admins** tab to grant and revoke access.
- Staff can time out or suspend a player for one minute to one year, or lift a restriction, from that player's profile options. Staff can restrict members. The super admin can also restrict admins. History records the staff member, reason, and expiry. Both restrictions block account writes immediately, including in sessions already open. The affected player can still browse and send a private message with **Contact staff** in the jump menu. Staff see these messages in their queue. Players can contact staff once every five minutes.
- **Pinned threads.** Admins and the owner can pin a thread to the top of its board. On a board or in a thread, press `o` for **thread options** (also in the jump menu) and choose **Pin to top of board** or **Unpin from board**. A board holds at most two pinned threads. Removing a thread releases its pin. Pinned threads stay above newer activity and show a `PINNED` chip in the board list, preview pane, thread header, and home screen's recent-thread list. The store checks the actor's role in the same transaction as the change.
- Threads and replies share a limit of five posts per key per rolling minute. Accounts less than 24 hours old share an additional cap of 20 posts per rolling minute. Removed and author-deleted posts still count. The database checks quotas in the posting transaction. Direct sessions also share a configurable cap of six per source network.
- **Live chat.** A message is one line of at most 500 characters. A key can send five messages per 10 seconds and 20 per rolling minute. Accounts less than 24 hours old share a cap of 60 per rolling minute. Deleted and removed messages still count. An author's **Delete message** clears the public message. A staff **Remove message** hides it and records the decision. Reports preserve a private snapshot for review even if the author deletes the message.
- **Formatting and links.** Bodies are stored exactly as typed and formatted only when drawn (see [04-rich-text-and-hover.md](04-rich-text-and-hover.md)), so moderation needs no migration and the queue still shows the raw text, including the real target behind a `[label](link)`. `@user`, `#board`, `#12` and `#12/3` become links only when the target exists, and one load resolves at most 64 of them. External links never open directly: they show a warning with the real host first, flagging hosts that mix alphabets.
- A later Arcade change could forward a lobby-issued network identifier accepted only from trusted proxy keys, or limit new identities at the lobby. For a sustained attack across many networks, invite-only posting would provide stronger control at the cost of open signup. Both require separate rollout decisions.
- SQLite migrates existing data in place. Back up the database before rollout. Restoring that backup is required to roll back to the old binary, which rejects newer schema versions.
